## Abstract

sc-ChromAging, a chromatin accessibility-based aging clock, was developed using single-cell ATAC-seq from 401 Chinese individuals. It identified CD4⁺ naive T cells as the most accurate predictors of age. This clock linked immune aging with pathways in inflammation, infection, and tumor susceptibility, and connecting chromatin changes to plasma metabolites like triacylglycerols.

Global population aging presents a profound public health challenge 1 , necessitating a deeper understanding of the biological deterioration that accompanies advancing age. The aging process in humans constitutes a complex progression that exerts widespread effects across various organ systems, with the immune system displaying particularly significant dysregulation. This deterioration of immune integrity, often termed immunosenescence 2 , is intrinsically linked to an attenuated capacity for tissue regeneration, a heightened vulnerability to infectious diseases, and the disruption of systemic homeostasis, all of which facilitate the pathogenesis of age-associated morbidities 3 – 5 . While chronological age serves as a rough proxy for these changes, it often fails to capture the substantial heterogeneity in health trajectories among individuals 6 . Consequently, the quantification of biological age through molecular biomarkers has emerged as a pivotal strategy to assess aging status and predict health outcomes 7 – 10 . Among the hallmarks of aging, epigenetic remodeling is considered a primary driver of the aging process 11 .

The concept of an epigenetic clock was pioneered using DNA methylation data 12 . However, the majority of existing DNA methylation clocks rely on bulk tissue profiles 13 , which makes it difficult to discern whether observed changes arise from alterations within specific cells. Chromatin accessibility, measured by single-cell ATAC-sequencing (scATAC-seq), reflects the regulatory potential of the genome 14 . As an upstream layer, chromatin state provides unique mechanistic insights into how the aging process rewires the regulatory network of immune cells, yet high-resolution clocks based on scATAC-seq remain unexplored.

Furthermore, the generalizability of existing single-cell aging clocks is limited by a demographic bias, as current studies predominantly focus on individuals of European ancestry 8 . Immune aging is a complex phenotype shaped by the interplay between genetic background and environmental factors which vary significantly across populations 15 , 16 .

To decode the epigenetic heterogeneity of immune aging, the cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility aging clock sc-ChromAging were constructed using a high-quality scATAC-seq dataset derived from the Chinese Immune Multi-Omics Atlas (CIMA) cohort (Fig. 1a ) 17 , establishing a population-specific epigenetic baseline. The predictive performance of sc-ChromAging was evaluated across five major immune cell types. Significant heterogeneity in predictive performance was observed, and the CD4 + T cells exhibited the highest predictive accuracy (Fig. 1b ). Specifically, the categorical boosting (CatBoost)-based clock for CD4 + T cells demonstrated superior robustness (R 2  = 0.79, R = 0.90) (Fig. 1c ). The extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost)-based clock for CD8 + T & unconventional T cells achieved an R 2 of 0.66 (R = 0.83) (Fig. 1d ). Moreover, weaker predictive performance was detected for myeloid cells (R 2  = 0.41, R = 0.67), innate lymphoid cells (ILCs) (R 2  = 0.37, R = 0.63), and B cells (R 2  = 0.35, R = 0.61) (Fig. 1e-g ).

To further investigate the epigenetic signatures of aging at higher granularity, the analysis was extended to 25 immune cell subtypes. Consistent with the lineage-level findings, subtypes within the T cells displayed higher predictive accuracy (Fig. 2a-c ). Notably, CD4 + naive T cells showed the highest accuracy among subtypes with an R 2 of 0.78 (R = 0.89) (Fig. 2d ), followed by CD8 + naive T cells (R 2  = 0.68, R = 0.84) and CD4 + helper cells (R 2  = 0.65, R = 0.81) (Fig. 2e-f ). In contrast, other immune cell subtypes generally showed relatively lower predictive accuracy (Figure S1a-v ).

Mapping the identified age-associated chromatin accessibility peaks to their putative target genes revealed a high degree of cell-type specificity, with a substantial fraction of epigenetic signatures unique to individual cell types (Fig. 3a ). The top five cell subtypes ranked by R² were selected for further analysis. Despite the distinct epigenetic signatures across these cell types, their mapped target genes converged on largely shared pathways. Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis demonstrated that these age-associated features were predominantly concentrated in pathways related to inflammation, infections, and tumor susceptibility (Fig. 3b-d , Figure S2a-b ). Notably, beyond these shared signatures, CD4 + naive T cells exhibited a unique enrichment in metabolic signals, specifically in fatty acid biosynthesis, fatty acid metabolism, and cyclic AMP (cAMP) signaling pathway (Fig. 3b ). Representative loci further illustrated age-dependent accessibility changes, with selected peaks showing a decline in mean accessibility across age in CD4⁺ naive T cells (rho = -0.480, P  < 0.001), CD8⁺ naive T cells (rho = -0.344, P  < 0.001), CD4 + helper T cells (rho = -0.226, P  < 0.001), CD8⁺ memory T cells (rho = -0.305, P  < 0.001), and MAIT cells (rho = -0.237, P  < 0.001) (Fig. 3e-g , Figure S2c-d ).

To investigate whether the chromatin remodeling captured by sc-ChromAging reflects systemic physiological states, the scATAC-derived epigenetic age acceleration (EAA) of CD4⁺ naive T cells was integrated with the matched plasma metabolomics and lipidomics profiles. Consistent with the enrichment of fatty acid biosynthesis pathways in the chromatin data (Fig. 3b ), the EAA showed a significant correlation with multiple triacylglycerols (TAGs) (Fig. 4a-d ). This concordance indicated that the upregulated fatty acid synthesis in epigenetically accelerated CD4⁺ naive T cells might couple with systemic lipid accumulation.

In this study, sc-ChromAging revealed that immune aging was fundamentally heterogeneous, with different immune lineages following distinct aging trajectories, ranging from highly predictable patterns to largely stochastic changes. This research addressed a key gap by being the first scATAC-seq based clock conducted on a Chinese cohort.

The relatively high predictive accuracy observed in CD4 + naive T cells suggested that their chromatin landscape may effectively reflect the biological aging process. Mechanistically, this high precision may be related to the intrinsic program of thymic involution 18 . Unlike memory or effector subsets whose epigenomes are mainly remodeled by antigen exposure 19 , 20 , naive T cells may maintain a relatively quiescent state where chromatin accessibility changes are driven primarily by the intrinsic aging program. Notably, although CD8⁺ naive T cells also showed relatively good predictive performance, their accuracy remained lower than that of CD4⁺ naive T cells. This distinction suggests that a quiescent phenotype alone does not necessarily confer the same degree of age predictability across naive T-cell compartments. One possible explanation is that the chromatin state of CD8⁺ naive T cells may be more susceptible to extrinsic regulatory influences associated with their survival and maintenance, including cytokine-dependent homeostatic signals and other environmental stimuli 21 . Such influences may introduce greater inter-individual heterogeneity in chromatin accessibility patterns, thereby reducing the consistency of age-associated epigenetic changes captured by the model. Furthermore, a minimal age-associated chromatin remodeling was observed in naive B cells and innate myeloid cells. Chromatin accessibility reflected the activity of gene regulation, and the low predictive accuracy observed in these cells suggested that they may be influenced by environmental factors, which could obscure the signals of aging.

Moreover, the findings provide a necessary cell-type-specific contextualization to prior foundational work which identified memory CD8⁺ T cells as the primary drivers of aging-associated chromatin accessibility changes using bulk ATAC-seq 22 . While this may initially seem to contradict the observation that CD4⁺ naive T cells yield the most accurate aging clock, the divergence stems from the different analytical frameworks employed. The previous study focused on the magnitude of differential accessibility, where memory CD8⁺ T cells exhibit extensive remodeling due to cumulative lifelong antigen exposures 22 . In contrast, the machine learning-based clock in this study prioritizes predictability and consistency. Even though CD4⁺ naive T cells exhibit less chromatin remodeling overall, their epigenetic erosion is driven by a strictly conserved, intrinsic aging program, thereby serving as an optimal substrate for a highly accurate predictive clock.

Compared to transcriptomic approaches, the scATAC-clock had the advantage of focusing on upstream regulatory factors, as chromatin accessibility reflects the potential for gene regulation rather than just its functional output. The age-associated loss of accessibility at specific peaks identified in the model signified a reduction in regulatory potential. To further corroborate the biological validity of the selected chromatin features, enrichment analysis was performed on putative target genes mapped by genomic proximity to the top-ranked age-related peaks. Notably, these genomic loci were predominantly enriched in pathways associated with inflammation, infections, and tumorigenesis. The enrichment of infection-related pathways likely reflected the cumulative epigenetic changes resulting from life-long pathogen exposure 23 , 24 , while the emergence of cancer-related signaling in these healthy individuals did not imply malignancy, but rather pointed to a shared molecular mechanism between aging and oncogenesis 25 . Significantly, the multi-omics integration extended these findings beyond local cellular regulation to systemic physiological states. The strong concordance between the enrichment of fatty acid biosynthesis pathways, as inferred from chromatin accessibility profiles, and the accumulation of plasma TAGs in epigenetically accelerated individuals suggests that sc-ChromAging captures a state of systemic metabolic reprogramming.

Establishing this baseline for the Chinese population is scientifically crucial. Given the known disparities in pathogen exposure history and lifestyle factors between East Asian and Western populations 26 , 27 , there were differences in their immune cells 28 . This study enabled the comparison of population-specific aging markers, shedding light on how these factors influence the aging process.

Despite these insights, several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, while chromatin accessibility is a robust metric for regulatory potential, it captures only one dimension of the complex aging phenotype. It did not capture the full complexity of aging in all lineages. Future studies should aim to integrate multi-modal data, combining scATAC-seq with scRNA-seq and single-cell V(D)J sequencing. Second, due to the current scarcity of comparable large-scale scATAC-seq datasets for East Asian populations, independent external validation was not feasible in this study, necessitating future multi-center collaborations. Finally, while sc-ChromAging has achieved high precision in CD4 + T cells, a portion of the variance remains unexplained. This residual variance likely reflects the impact of non-temporal factors such as individual genetic polymorphisms and distinct environmental exposures. Future iterations of the aging clock should therefore aim to incorporate polygenic risk scores and exposome data to further elucidate the multifaceted drivers of biological aging 29 .

In summary, this study established the first single-cell chromatin accessibility aging clock, sc-ChromAging, for the Chinese population, decoding the epigenetic heterogeneity of immunosenescence across healthy individuals. These findings not only provide a population-specific metric for quantifying biological age but also offer mechanistic insights into the erosion of immune integrity, paving the way for precise monitoring of aging process and the development of targeted epigenetic interventions to rejuvenate the aging immune system.

## Methods

## Study cohorts

The scATAC-seq data of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), and plasma metabolomics and lipidomics were accessed from the CIMA ( https://db.cngb.org/trueblood/cima/resource ), which is a systematic analysis of blood components from 428 Chinese adults without active diseases 17 . A total of 401 individuals with scATAC-seq data were included in this study. PBMC suspensions were barcoded for scATAC-seq using the DNBelab C Series Single-Cell Library Preparation platform, and 100,000 to 500,000 cells were fixed with 0.01% formaldehyde followed by cell lysis and tagmentation using the DNBelab C Series High-throughput Single-cell ATAC Library Preparation Set V1.0 (MGI, no. 940-000793-00). All libraries were sequenced on the DNBSEQ platform. The sequencing strategy for scATAC-seq libraries were sequenced with a 70 bp Read 1 and a 50 bp Read 2. The processed h5ad file was downloaded for analysis. Targeted metabolomics was performed using the HM Meta Assay (BGI, Shenzhen, China), capable of quantifying approximately 700 polar metabolites across diverse chemical classes, including amino acids, fatty acids, bile acids, carboxylic acids, and indoles. Data acquisition was performed in UPLC-MRM mode with electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative polarities. Lipidomic profiling was conducted using the HM Lipid Assay (BGI, Shenzhen, China), targeting over 1600 lipid species across 18 major subclasses, including TAGs, diacylglycerols (DAGs), ceramides (Cer), and various phospholipids. To optimize coverage, lipid species were detected in specific ionization modes. SMs, CEs, Cer, TAGs, DAGs, and MAGs were quantified in positive mode, while phospholipids and lysophospholipids were quantified in negative mode using scheduled MRM settings. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Recruitment and study design for this study were approved by the Institutional Review Board of BGI (BGI-IRB 21157-T3). This study was conducted in accordance with the declaration of Helsinki 30 .

## Data preprocessing and cell type annotation

The CIMA was used in model training and testing. To ensure robust model generalization and prevent data leakage, the dataset was partitioned into training (80%) and validation (20%) sets using a donor-level stratified shuffle split strategy, which balanced age distributions across splits. While the original annotation results comprise four hierarchical levels, this study primarily characterizes cell identities at the first two levels, designated as Level 1 for major lineages and Level 2 for detailed cell subtypes. At Level 1, immune cells were classified into five major groups, including CD4 + T cells (CD4 T), CD4 + and unconventional T cells (CD8 & unconventional T), B cells (B), myeloid cells (Myeloid), and ILC. At Level 2, these major lineages were further resolved into specific subtypes based on distinct chromatin accessibility signatures, including CD4 + naive T cells, CD4 + memory T cells, CD4 + helper T cells, CD4 + regulatory T cells, CD4 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD4 CTL), CD8 + naive T cells, CD8 + memory T cells, CD8 + CTL (CD8 CTL), proliferative T cells, gamma-delta T cells (gdT), mucosal-associated invariant T cells (MAIT), natural killer T cells (NKT), transitional T cells, naive B cells, memory B cells, total plasma cells, transitional B cells, megakaryocytes (MK), dendritic cells (DC), plasmacytoid DCs (pDC), monocytes (Mono), group 2 ILCs (ILC2), CD56 + bright natural killer cells (CD56 + NK bright ), transitional natural killer cells, CD56 + dim natural killer cells (CD56 + NK dim ), and proliferative natural killer cells.

## Cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility aging clock

A dataset consisting of 401 individuals was employed to train and valid the model, with the chronological age being used as the reference standard. Cell-type-specific aging clocks were developed to predict chronological age from scATAC-seq profiles across diverse human immune cell types. This was achieved by selecting cell-type-specific marker peaks that exhibited age-associated changes in chromatin accessibility. The hypothesis is held that the biological aging process is mirrored by the dynamic remodeling of chromatin accessibility at these specific regulatory loci, which is strongly correlated with the passage of time. By the chromatin accessibility aging clock, upstream epigenetic mechanisms of aging are illuminated via the identification of specific marker peaks, which helps understanding the regulatory landscapes of aging and age-related diseases.

## Feature selection

Feature selection was performed exclusively on the training set to identify robust cell-type-specific age-associated chromatin accessibility peaks. The aggregated pseudo-bulk accessibility of each peak for each individual was first calculated and correlated with chronological age using Spearman rank correlation. Features with a P value > 0.1 was filtered out to remove non-informative noise. Recognizing that linear correlation might miss complex biological signals, the features were further evaluated using mutual information regression and random forest feature importance on a balanced subsample of 10,000 cells to capture non-linear relationships. To integrate the informative feature lists identified by these three distinct metrics, the non-parametric rank product method was applied. The ranked features were permuted 1000 times to generate a null distribution and the rank product of each peak was calculated across the permuted matrices. The probability of false prediction (PFP) was then estimated by comparing the observed rank product to the permuted distribution, and the top 300 features were selected to ensure optimal input dimensionality for the aging clock models.

## Converting scATAC-seq into pseudo-cells

To counteract the high sparsity associated with scATAC-seq data, a pseudo-cell aggregation strategy based on bootstrap resampling was implemented to bolster signal stability. Crucially, to strictly prevent potential data leakage, the dataset was partitioned into training and validation cohorts strictly at the donor level prior to pseudo-cell generation. For every specific donor and cell type combination, 200 pseudo-replicates were constructed by computing the mean accessibility profile of 100 randomly sampled single cells. Sampling with replacement was permitted exclusively when the total cell count fell below the sampling threshold.

## Model establishment and evaluation criteria

To identify the optimal regression strategy for predicting chronological age, four distinct machine learning algorithms, including XGBoost, light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM), CatBoost, and a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) neural network, were evaluated. Specifically, the MLP architecture comprising three hidden layers with ReLU activation was implemented to capture complex non-linear dependencies. To mitigate bias arising from the uneven age distribution in the dataset, a weighting scheme based on the inverse frequency of each age group was applied during the training of models. Feature scaling was performed using standardization prior to training the MLP to optimize convergence. All models were trained on the pseudo-cell aggregated data, and their predictive accuracy was assessed on the validation set using the coefficient of determination (R 2 ), mean absolute error (MAE), and root mean square error (RMSE).

## Robustness evaluation and consensus feature identification

To ensure the reproducibility of predictive performance and identify stable age-associated peaks, a repeated stratified split-validation framework was implemented. This approach was designed to mitigate potential biases by first randomly partitioning the samples into training and validation sets, and then performing single-cell subsampling. The entire analytical pipeline, including sample-level stratified dataset splitting (80% training vs. 20% validation), balanced cellular subsampling, and model training, was executed three independent times using distinct random seeds. For model evaluation, the final performance metrics were reported as the mean values across the three independent validation cohorts. For feature selection, the age-associated peaks identified in each run were intersected to obtain a robust set of consensus features. Only peaks consistently selected across all three runs were retained as key age-associated signatures for downstream functional enrichment analyses.

## Gene mapping and enrichment analysis

To functionally characterize the age-associated chromatin accessibility peaks identified by feature selection pipeline, genomic coordinates were mapped to their nearest regulatory target genes. The GRCh38 human reference genome annotation (GENCODE release 41) was used to define gene boundaries and transcription start sites (TSS). Specifically, for each peak, the linear genomic distance to the TSS of all protein-coding genes located on the same chromosome was calculated. Each peak was subsequently assigned to the gene with the minimum absolute distance between the peak center and the gene TSS. Peaks mapping to genomic scaffolds without annotated gene features were excluded from downstream functional enrichment analyses. The KEGG enrichment analysis was then conducted using clusterProfiler R package 31 .

## Plasma metabolites association analysis

To evaluate whether chromatin accessibility–based immune aging signals are reflected at the systemic metabolic level, scATAC-derived age predictions were integrated with matched plasma metabolomics and lipidomics data at the individual level 17 . To adjust for the confounding effect of chronological age, the EAA was calculated using the standard residual method. An ordinary least squares (OLS) linear regression model was fitted with the scATAC-derived predicted age as the dependent variable and chronological age as the independent variable. The resulting residuals were defined as the EAA. Finally, for each metabolite, a non-parametric Spearman rank correlation analysis was performed to assess its association with the EAA across individuals.

## Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed by R 4.3.1 ( http://www.R-project.org ) and Python 3.9 ( https://www.python.org/ ). The training set and validation set were randomly selected. The python library lightgbm, xgboost, catboost,and scikit-learn were used for model construction. A threshold of P  < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all the statistical tests.
