## Abstract

As AI virtual humans become increasingly prominent communicative actors on short-video platforms, digital kinship content characterized by kinship-based forms of address, family narratives, caring expressions, and companionship-oriented interaction has begun to enter older adults’ everyday media use and become intertwined with commercial recommendation. Selectively drawing on the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism and integrating it with the Computers Are Social Actors (CASA) paradigm, this study conceptualizes loneliness as a manifestation of sociality motivation; emotional appeal, human likeness, and homophily as perceived cues and appraisals associated with elicited agent knowledge; and social presence and empathy as proximal social responses within the CASA framework. It examines older adults’ social perceptions of and emotional responses to kinship-oriented AI virtual humans and their associations with purchase intention. Using a standardized set of AI virtual human short videos representing key features of digital kinship content as stimuli, we collected valid data from 372 Chinese adults aged 65 years and older and employed partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) as parallel and complementary analytical approaches. The PLS-SEM results revealed distinct antecedent patterns for the two proximal responses. Emotional appeal showed the strongest association with social presence, whereas homophily was more strongly associated with empathy than with social presence. Human likeness was significantly associated only with social presence, while loneliness was positively associated with both responses. Empathy showed a stronger association with purchase intention than social presence, and all indirect associations were significant except the pathway from human likeness through empathy. The fsQCA results further showed that the combination of emotional appeal and empathy constituted the most stable configuration associated with high purchase intention, whereas loneliness emerged as a core condition only when homophily was also present. This study provides empirical evidence for understanding perceptions of social presence and empathy in later-life human–AI relational perception and offers practical implications for the governance of kinship-oriented AI commercial content and the protection of older consumers.

 Keywords: AI virtual humans, anthropomorphism, digital kinship, empathy, loneliness, older adults, social presence

## Introduction

The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence has brought AI virtual humans, characterized by humanlike appearance, language, and behavioral features, into increasingly prominent communicative roles on short-video platforms ( Thomas and Fowler, 2021 ). Compared with conventional digital characters, AI virtual humans can maintain relatively stable identities over time and participate in information dissemination and commercial communication through natural language, emotional expression, and social interaction, thereby creating more socially engaging forms of mediated experience ( Liu et al., 2025 ).

As population aging and digitalization increasingly intersect, such content is also becoming part of older adults’ everyday media use. In China, older adults have already adopted short-video platforms for information seeking, informal learning, and socioemotional experiences ( Qian et al., 2025 ). At the same time, kinship-role AI-generated influencers specifically targeting older audiences have begun to emerge on these platforms. Through forms of address such as “uncle and auntie” and “older sister,” family-centered narratives, daily greetings, and expressions of care, these virtual characters create a kinship-like communicative atmosphere while embedding health-related information, everyday stories, and product recommendations into their content ( Song et al., 2026 ). As AI virtual humans enter commercial communication through family roles and kinship-oriented interaction, an important question arises: which user-level psychological conditions and virtual-character cues are associated with older adults’ social responses in such kinship-oriented AI communication contexts, and how are these responses related to purchase intention?

We refer to this communicative environment, characterized by kinship roles, family narratives, expressions of care, and companionship-oriented interaction, as a digital kinship context. This phenomenon extends the long-standing relational practices that have developed between media figures and audiences ( Horton and Wohl, 1956 ), while also introducing new features enabled by generative AI. AI allows content creators to produce virtual characters at scale while maintaining stable personas, consistent linguistic styles, and sustained emotional expression, making it possible to incorporate kinship-oriented communication into short-video content at lower cost and with greater frequency ( Song et al., 2026 ). For older users, such content simultaneously combines cues of social connection, emotional information, and commercial recommendation. Kinship-oriented expression may therefore provide a sense of companionship while also blurring the boundary between emotional support and commercial persuasion. Its implications cannot be fully understood through product information or technology use alone and raise new questions regarding older consumer protection and the disclosure of AI identity.

Existing research provides an important foundation for understanding this phenomenon, but three areas warrant further attention. First, research on older adults’ digital media use has largely focused on the digital divide, technology adoption, platform dependence, and the consequences of short-video use, while paying relatively limited attention to how AI-generated media figures enter older adults’ social and relational processing ( Xu et al., 2025 ). Recent work has begun to examine kinship-role AI-generated influencers and the forms of support, interaction, and risk that older adults may experience in relation to them ( Song et al., 2026 ), yet the psychological processes involved remain to be systematically examined.

Second, research on virtual influencers and AI-mediated characters has primarily focused on younger users and on contexts such as brand endorsement, virtual livestreaming, and influencer marketing, with substantial attention to anthropomorphism, authenticity, social presence, and purchase intention ( Kim and Park, 2023 ; Liu et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ). Much of this work has examined cues originating from the virtual character while giving less consideration to users’ own state of social connectedness. This omission is particularly important for older adults. Socioemotional selectivity theory suggests that as perceived future time becomes more limited, older adults place greater value on emotionally meaningful relationships and information ( Carstensen et al., 1999 ). Whether their need for social connection is fulfilled may therefore shape how they interpret AI virtual humans presented through family roles. Accordingly, older adults’ loneliness should be examined within the same framework as the human likeness, emotional appeal, and homophily of AI virtual humans.

Third, research on livestream shopping and e-commerce has established that psychological factors such as social presence and emotional experience are closely associated with consumer intentions ( Jiang and Chen, 2024 ; Jin et al., 2025 ). However, different forms of social response in AI virtual human contexts have yet to be clearly distinguished. Social presence captures the extent to which users experience a virtual character as a socially present actor, whereas empathy involves state-based understanding of and emotional responsiveness to the character’s emotions and situation during media exposure ( Lee, 2004 ; Shen, 2010b ). Distinguishing these two proximal responses makes it possible to identify which cues make a virtual character feel socially present, which cues facilitate emotional understanding, and how each response is separately associated with purchase intention.

To explain this process, the present study selectively draws on the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism and integrates it with the CASA paradigm. The three-factor theory proposes that whether individuals attribute human characteristics to nonhuman agents depends on three factors: elicited agent knowledge, effectance motivation, and sociality motivation ( Epley et al., 2007 ). The present study focuses only on sociality motivation and elicited agent knowledge. Loneliness is conceptualized as a manifestation of sociality motivation; human likeness and emotional appeal are treated as perceived cues centered on AI virtual humans; and homophily is treated as an appraisal of similarity between the self and the AI virtual human. All three are theoretically associated with elicited agent knowledge. The CASA paradigm further suggests that when technological objects provide sufficient social information, users draw on social rules derived from real-world interpersonal interaction when responding to them ( Nass and Moon, 2000 ). Accordingly, this study focuses on social presence and empathy as two proximal social responses and examines their associations with purchase intention.

Methodologically, this study employed a standardized stimulus-assisted cross-sectional survey design. Before completing the questionnaire, all older participants viewed the same set of AI virtual human short videos in a fixed order, ensuring that subsequent perceptual evaluations were made within a consistent digital kinship communication context. Emotional appeal, human likeness, and homophily were measured as participants’ post-exposure perceptions and appraisals of the standardized stimuli. The stimulus materials were used primarily to establish a common research context, and these characteristics were not experimentally manipulated. We then employed partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) as two parallel and complementary analytical approaches. PLS-SEM follows a symmetric net-effect logic and is used to estimate the average and indirect associations among the study variables and purchase intention. In contrast, fsQCA adopts a set-theoretic and causally asymmetric logic to examine whether high purchase intention can arise from multiple combinations of conditions and whether conditions with relatively weak net associations in SEM become important within particular configurations. Together, these approaches address two complementary questions: which factors matter on average, and under what combinations of conditions are they jointly associated with high purchase intention.
 2. 
## Theoretical framework
 2.1. 
## Seeing AI virtual humans as social others: the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism

Anthropomorphism refers to the process by which individuals attribute human characteristics, intentions, motivations, or emotions to nonhuman entities and interpret their behavior through human social-cognitive frameworks ( Epley et al., 2007 ; Waytz et al., 2010 ). The three-factor theory of anthropomorphism proposed by Epley et al. (2007) identifies three primary determinants of anthropomorphic perception: elicited agent knowledge, effectance motivation, and sociality motivation. Elicited agent knowledge concerns the extent to which individuals draw on existing knowledge about humans and human social schemas when interpreting nonhuman agents. Cues related to human appearance, behavior, and forms of expression increase the accessibility of such schemas ( Epley et al., 2007 ; Waytz et al., 2010 ). Effectance motivation arises from the need to understand, predict, and control an agent’s behavior; when an entity is relatively uncertain or difficult to interpret, people are more likely to explain it in terms of human intentions and mental states ( Epley et al., 2007 ). Sociality motivation, by contrast, stems from the need to establish and maintain social connection. When social connection is insufficient or the motivation for social approach is stronger, individuals are more likely to attribute social qualities to nonhuman entities ( Epley et al., 2008b ; Dang and Liu, 2024 ).

AI virtual humans possess humanlike appearances, linguistic expressions, emotional displays, and relatively stable character identities. Their social perception may therefore be shaped both by the humanlike cues presented by the agent and by users’ own need for social connection. Existing research indicates that human likeness, behavioral naturalness, and social interaction cues influence how users perceive the humanness and social qualities of virtual agents ( Blut et al., 2021 ; Zourrig et al., 2025 ). At the same time, individuals’ state of social connectedness can also shape their tendency to anthropomorphize nonhuman agents ( Epley et al., 2008a ; Dang and Liu, 2024 ).

Accordingly, the present study selectively draws on the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism, focusing on two of its three determinants, sociality motivation and elicited agent knowledge. This framework provides a theoretical basis for distinguishing user-level motivational conditions from perceptual cues that make human-related knowledge accessible. Sociality motivation captures individual differences in audiences’ need for social connection, whereas elicited agent knowledge concerns the human-related and self-related knowledge that viewers may draw on when interpreting AI virtual humans. Effectance motivation was not included in the present model. This factor is particularly relevant when an agent’s behavior is uncertain and must be understood and predicted ( Epley et al., 2007 ). In the present study, all virtual characters were explicitly labeled as AI-generated and were presented through the same scripted narratives to all participants. The research design neither manipulated nor captured the uncertainty on which effectance motivation depends; accordingly, effectance motivation was not incorporated into the theoretical model.
 2.2. 
## Responding to AI virtual humans as social actors: the CASA paradigm

Once a nonhuman agent acquires sufficient social meaning, a further question concerns how individuals respond to it. The Computers Are Social Actors (CASA) paradigm proposes that when technological systems display cues that are recognizable as social, people transfer the rules, norms, and judgment processes of interpersonal interaction to their interactions with technology ( Nass et al., 1994 ; Reeves and Nass, 1996 ). Early experiments showed that users exhibited interpersonal responses toward computers, including politeness, reciprocity, personality matching, gender stereotyping, and group identification, even when they were fully aware that they were interacting with a computer ( Nass et al., 1994 , 1999 ; Nass and Moon, 2000 ).

The CASA paradigm was subsequently extended to a broader range of human–technology interactions. Virtual agents with personalized interfaces and embodied features have been shown to elicit social judgments and behaviors similar to those observed in interpersonal settings ( Verhagen et al., 2014 ). In text-based chatbots, linguistic style, identity cues, and anthropomorphic design likewise shape users’ evaluations of the agent’s social qualities and interaction quality ( Feine et al., 2019 ; Seeger et al., 2021 ). Research on social robots further shows that vocal and interactional cues can influence users’ perceptions of social presence and their interaction experience ( Xu, 2019 ; Edwards et al., 2019 ). Together, these studies suggest that the explanatory scope of CASA depends on whether a technology provides sufficiently salient social cues to trigger social processing and prompt users to apply familiar interpersonal rules.

AI virtual humans typically possess stable character identities, facial and bodily representations, distinctive linguistic styles, and emotional expressions, while also participating in ongoing narratives and interactions through social media. These features allow them to carry a richer set of social cues than conventional interfaces. Prior research indicates that users form social evaluations of virtual humans based on cues such as emotional expression, anthropomorphism, and perceived authenticity, which in turn shape their attitudes and interaction-related responses ( Ham et al., 2024 ). CASA therefore provides a useful theoretical basis for understanding how older adults incorporate AI virtual humans into a social interaction framework. From this perspective, audiences respond to virtual characters at relational, emotional, and behavioral levels according to the social cues they perceive.

Social presence and empathy thus constitute the two proximal social responses examined in this study. Although they may co-occur within the same mediated experience, they are conceptually distinct. Social presence reflects users’ perception of the virtual character as a genuinely present social actor ( Short et al., 1976 ; Lee, 2004 ; Biocca et al., 2003 ), whereas empathy captures state-based cognitive and emotional responses to the character’s condition, situation, and emotions during media processing ( Shen, 2010b ; Cuff et al., 2016 ; Pedersen, 2021 ). Although they may co-occur within the same mediated experience, the two constructs capture conceptually distinct forms of psychological response. Accordingly, this study models social presence and empathy as two parallel proximal social responses.
 2.3. 
## Digital kinship as context: an integrated framework

Older adults’ use of short-video platforms in China has gradually expanded beyond information seeking to include informal learning, companionship, and socioemotional experience, making these platforms an increasingly important environment for everyday information use and relational engagement ( Qian et al., 2025 ). At the same time, kinship-role AI-generated influencers targeting older audiences have begun to emerge on Chinese short-video platforms. Through their character design, kinship-based forms of address, family narratives, and sustained dialogue, these virtual figures take on family roles such as children, siblings, or grandchildren and invite users into interactions framed in kinship terms ( Song et al., 2026 ). In this study, we define digital kinship as an AI-mediated communication context constituted by kinship roles, family narratives, expressions of care, and companionship-oriented interaction. Within this context, AI virtual humans communicate through the identities and interactional norms associated with family relationships, placing information, emotional exchange, and product recommendation within the same relational frame.

In this study, the concept of digital kinship is used in a deliberately limited sense. It refers to a category of AI-generated short-video content and the communicative context constituted by that content, and is therefore situated at the level of content and context rather than viewers’ psychological states. Digital kinship itself was not measured, nor does this study seek to establish it as an independent psychological construct or relationship type. Because digital kinship operates at the contextual level, it is analytically distinct from several related constructs. Parasocial interaction and parasocial relationships describe audiences’ one-sided responses to and attachments toward media figures ( Horton and Wohl, 1956 ); social presence and empathy capture viewers’ perceptual and emotional responses to social agents ( Zhang et al., 2024 ; Li Q. et al., 2023 ); and anthropomorphism refers to the general process of attributing human characteristics to nonhuman entities. These psychological responses may arise across a range of settings, whereas digital kinship specifies one particular communicative context distinguished by the use of family roles as a template for communication. The present study therefore differs from general virtual-influencer research primarily in the context it examines. Empirically, digital kinship was operationalized as a stimulus category: all participants viewed the same set of videos selected on the basis of the defining features described above (see Section 4.2) and completed all subsequent measures with reference to the characters presented in those videos.

The analytical framework of this study combines a selective application of the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism with the CASA paradigm. The former draws on sociality motivation and elicited agent knowledge to explain how AI virtual humans acquire social meaning ( Epley et al., 2007 ), whereas the latter explains how users respond to them socially and how such responses are related to behavioral intentions ( Nass and Moon, 2000 ). Accordingly, the model is organized into four levels: the user-level motivational condition represented by loneliness; the perceived cues and appraisals represented by human likeness, emotional appeal, and homophily; the proximal social responses represented by social presence and empathy; and the behavioral intention represented by purchase intention.
 3. 
## Research hypotheses
 3.1. 
## Sociality motivation and loneliness

Loneliness arises from a discrepancy between individuals’ actual and desired social relationships and fundamentally reflects an unmet need for social connection ( Cacioppo and Patrick, 2008 ). Within the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism, this need corresponds to sociality motivation. When real-world social connection is insufficient, individuals may become more inclined to seek socially interpretable and relationally meaningful targets among nonhuman agents ( Epley et al., 2007 ). Experimental research has shown that both chronically lonely individuals and those whose loneliness is situationally induced are more likely to attribute human characteristics to nonhuman entities such as pets and technological devices, suggesting that insufficient social connection can increase the tendency to compensate through nonhuman social targets ( Epley et al., 2008a , b ).

This tendency toward social connection has particular relevance in older adults’ media use. Lim and Kim (2011) found that lonelier older television-shopping viewers were more likely to develop parasocial interaction with on-screen hosts, indicating that older adults experiencing loneliness may incorporate media figures into their social-relational processing even in commercial settings. Loneliness is also associated with heightened vigilance toward social threat and with distrust and withdrawal in face-to-face interaction ( Cacioppo and Hawkley, 2009 ). Such defensive tendencies are especially relevant when potential rejection is involved. By contrast, socially excluded individuals may be more willing to seek reconnection with new targets that pose little risk of rejection ( Maner et al., 2007 ), while media figures can provide a sense of belonging without requiring reciprocal interaction or exposing users to interpersonal rejection ( Derrick et al., 2009 ). In the digital kinship context examined here, interaction with AI virtual humans is largely one-sided: older viewers are not required to respond and are not subject to evaluation, while kinship-based forms of address, caring expressions, family narratives, and companionship-oriented language communicate cues of acceptance ( Song et al., 2026 ). These features may make loneliness-related sociality motivation more likely to take the form of approach, increasing the likelihood that older adults experience the virtual character as a socially present actor.

Loneliness may also shape how individuals process socioemotional information. Lonely individuals are particularly attentive to cues signaling acceptance, rejection, and emotional states, reflecting heightened monitoring of socially relevant information ( Cacioppo and Hawkley, 2009 ; Gardner et al., 2005 ). This sensitivity appears to be selective, suggesting that loneliness may shape the kinds of people and situations toward which individuals direct empathic engagement ( Gagner et al., 2025 ). Two experiments by Hu et al. (2020) illustrate this selectivity. Compared with nonlonely individuals, lonely participants were more willing to engage in empathy tasks involving positive emotional contexts and were less likely to select negatively valenced empathy situations; this preference was explained by the perceived social support associated with positive empathy. When another person’s emotional state conveys acceptance and support, lonely individuals may therefore be more inclined to understand and share that experience as a means of meeting unmet relational needs. In the present context, AI virtual humans use kinship-based forms of address, everyday greetings, and expressions of care to convey companionship and support. Under such conditions, older adults experiencing greater loneliness may be more inclined to engage with the virtual character’s emotions and situation and to experience stronger understanding and emotional resonance.

Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

 H1 : Loneliness is positively associated with social presence.

 H2 : Loneliness is positively associated with empathy.
 3.2. 
## Perceived cues and appraisals associated with elicited agent knowledge

The three-factor theory of anthropomorphism proposes that elicited agent knowledge depends on whether knowledge about humans can be activated and applied to nonhuman entities. Such knowledge includes both general knowledge about humans and knowledge about the self ( Epley et al., 2007 ). Building on this view, the present study distinguishes three types of perceived cues and appraisals relevant to the social perception of AI virtual humans. Human likeness reflects the extent to which a virtual character resembles a real human in terms of animacy, perceived realness of existence, and behavioral naturalness, thereby making general knowledge about humans more readily accessible. Emotional appeal reflects the emotional states and relational intentions conveyed through self-disclosure, expressions of care, and companionship-oriented communication, providing viewers with information for interpreting the character’s psychological state. Homophily, in turn, reflects the extent to which viewers perceive similarity between themselves and the virtual character in values, ways of thinking, and life experiences, making self-knowledge more readily available as a reference point for understanding the other.

Homophily warrants further clarification. It is not an attribute of the AI virtual human itself, but a relational appraisal of similarity between the self and the other. Epley et al. (2007) suggest that perceived similarity between a perceiver and a target increases the applicability of self-knowledge when interpreting that target and can therefore serve as an important contextual determinant of elicited agent knowledge. Accordingly, this study treats homophily as a self-relevant appraisal that increases the accessibility of self-knowledge. This distinction also maintains a clear conceptual boundary between homophily, human likeness, and emotional appeal, and provides a basis for subsequently examining their respective associations with social presence and empathy.
 3.2.1. 
## Emotional appeal as an emotional cue of AI virtual humans

Emotional appeal refers to the interpersonal emotional information conveyed by AI virtual humans through expressions of care, concern, companionship, and emotion. Prior research has identified the emotional expression of artificial agents as an important social cue capable of eliciting social responses. Across three scenario-based experiments, Zhang et al. (2024) found that when AI recommendation chatbots used emotional text, emojis, and emotional images, users reported significantly higher levels of perceived humanness, social interactivity, and social presence. Social presence also mediated the relationships between emotional cues and both perceived humanness and social interactivity. These findings suggest that emotional information conveyed by artificial agents can strengthen users’ perceptions of their social presence and interactive qualities, consistent with the broader literature on social presence. In artificial-agent contexts, social language and interpersonal communication cues can enhance this sense of presence. In an experiment involving healthcare conversational agents, Li B. et al. (2023) and Li Q. et al. (2023) found that agents using humanlike verbal cues, including self-introductions, greetings, expressions of thanks, and suggestions, elicited significantly higher levels of social presence than agents using machine-like language; a higher degree of humanlike appearance produced a similar effect. Likewise, greetings, expressions of care and concern, and companionship-oriented communication in digital kinship videos provide sustained interpersonal emotional information. Higher emotional appeal is therefore expected to be associated with stronger social presence.

The relationship between emotional appeal and empathy can be understood through the processing of emotional information. State empathy involves understanding another person’s emotions, cognitively apprehending their situation, and experiencing corresponding emotional responses ( Shen, 2010a , b ). The emotional content expressed by a character therefore provides an important input for empathic processing. Research on artificial agents has shown that cues conveying emotional states can shape users’ socioemotional responses to those agents ( Ham et al., 2024 ). More recent chatbot research further indicates that emotional cues such as emojis can increase users’ willingness to adopt chatbot recommendations through empathy, suggesting that emotional expression by artificial agents can become incorporated into users’ empathic processing ( Yan et al., 2025 ).

Older adults’ information processing is also strongly oriented toward emotional goals. Socioemotional selectivity theory suggests that with age, individuals place greater value on emotionally meaningful and relationally significant information ( Carstensen et al., 1999 ) and prioritize information that supports emotion regulation and relationship goals ( Carstensen and Mikels, 2005 ). In digital kinship videos, expressions of loneliness, care, and requests for companionship provide viewers with explicit information about the character’s emotional state and relational orientation, enabling them to understand the character’s feelings and situation. Emotional appeal is therefore expected to be positively associated with empathy.

Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

 H3 : Emotional appeal is positively associated with social presence.

 H4 : Emotional appeal is positively associated with empathy.
 3.2.2. 
## Human likeness as a humanlike cue of AI virtual humans

Human likeness refers to the extent to which audiences perceive an AI virtual human as resembling a real person in terms of animacy, a sense of real existence, and behavioral naturalness. Research on anthropomorphism has long suggested that humanlike features in appearance, movement, and behavior increase the accessibility of human knowledge when people interpret nonhuman entities ( Epley et al., 2007 ). In human–computer interaction research, humanlike appearance and verbal cues have likewise been widely used to strengthen users’ perceptions of artificial agents as social entities. In an experiment with health care conversational agents, Li B. et al. (2023) and Li Q. et al. (2023) found that higher levels of humanlike appearance significantly increased perceived anthropomorphism and social presence; humanlike verbal cues produced effects in the same direction. Because social presence depends on whether users experience a mediated entity as a socially present interaction partner ( Lee, 2004 ), virtual characters with stronger animacy, a greater sense of real existence, and more natural and fluent behavior may be more readily processed within a human social-cognitive framework. Li B. et al. (2023) and Li Q. et al. (2023) further showed that increasing the degree of humanlike appearance from low to high significantly enhanced social presence.

There is also some experimental evidence linking human likeness to empathy in human–machine interaction. Riek et al. (2009) exposed participants to videos showing targets that varied from highly mechanical to more humanlike while undergoing harm and assessed responses using empathy measures and helping-related choices. Participants reported greater empathy toward robots that more closely resembled humans than toward more mechanical-looking robots, suggesting that the degree of human likeness can shape observers’ empathic responses.

Anthropomorphism theory provides a further explanation for this relationship. Epley et al. (2007) argue that as nonhuman entities become more humanlike in form and behavior, existing knowledge about humans becomes more accessible and more likely to be used in interpreting their actions and mental states. Research on mind perception further suggests that people judge whether an entity possesses a mind along two broad dimensions, agency and experience. Experience refers to the perceived capacity to feel states such as pleasure, pain, fear, and desire, and is closely related to whether an entity is seen as deserving concern and moral consideration ( Gray et al., 2007 ). Experimental evidence also indicates that increasing the anthropomorphic appearance of social robots can strengthen attributions of both agency and experiential capacity ( Spaccatini et al., 2023 ). Accordingly, when an AI virtual human is perceived as more alive, more real, and more behaviorally natural, viewers may be more likely to interpret its emotions and circumstances through a human psychological framework and to regard it as a social entity capable of feeling. For the AI virtual humans used in the present study, which express loneliness and a desire for companionship, stronger human likeness is therefore expected to be associated with greater empathy.

Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

 H5 : Human likeness is positively associated with social presence.

 H6 : Human likeness is positively associated with empathy.
 3.2.3. 
## Homophily as perceived similarity between viewers and AI virtual humans

Homophily refers to the extent to which audiences perceive themselves as similar to a media figure in terms of attitudes, values, life experiences, or social background ( McCroskey et al., 1975 ). One important function of similarity in communication is to increase the self-relevance of both the message and its source. Social comparison theory suggests that individuals are more likely to use comparable others as points of reference because the attitudes and experiences of similar others are more readily applied when interpreting their own circumstances and forming judgments ( Festinger, 1954 ). In the context of media figures, perceived similarity can also strengthen audiences’ social responses to the character. Stein et al. (2024) , for example, found that perceived self-similarity was significantly associated with parasocial interaction with both human and virtual influencers. In the present study, AI virtual humans typically appear as younger family members or other kinship-role figures, and homophily is reflected in perceived alignment in life experiences, attitudes, and value judgments. McCroskey et al. (1975) likewise conceptualized perceived homophily across multiple dimensions, including attitudes, morality, and background, indicating that similarity may rest on several social-cognitive bases. When a virtual character’s experiences and values more closely align with those of older viewers, its communication may become more self-relevant and more readily experienced as personally directed, thereby strengthening the perception of the character as socially present.

The association between homophily and empathy is more closely grounded in shared experience and perspective inference. Empathy requires individuals to understand another person’s emotions and circumstances, and perceived similarity can provide a familiar frame of reference for such understanding by allowing viewers to draw on their own experiences when inferring another’s psychological state. Epley et al. (2007) argue that people often begin with their own knowledge and experience when making inferences about others’ minds, and perceived similarity increases the applicability of self-knowledge in understanding another person. Research on virtual influencers similarly suggests that perceived homophily can strengthen social connection with virtual figures and is associated with stronger relational responses and greater receptiveness to their recommendations ( Stein et al., 2024 ; Dondapati and Dehury, 2024 ). In kinship-oriented communication contexts, shared life experiences, value judgments, and interests may carry even greater relational significance, enabling older adults to interpret the emotions and circumstances expressed by AI virtual humans through the lens of their own experiences. Higher homophily is therefore expected to be associated with stronger state empathy.

Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

 H7 : Homophily is positively associated with social presence.

 H8 : Homophily is positively associated with empathy.
 3.3. 
## Social presence, empathy, and purchase intention

Purchase intention reflects consumers’ subjective likelihood of purchasing a product or service in the future and is widely used as a behavioral-intention indicator of communication and marketing outcomes ( Dodds et al., 1991 ). In the context of AI virtual human recommendations, purchase intention may depend not only on product information itself but also on how users interpret the recommender as a social entity. CASA research suggests that once a technological object is incorporated into a social interaction framework, users may apply interpersonal norms and judgment rules, such as politeness, reciprocity, trust, and social evaluation, to their interactions with that technology ( Nass and Moon, 2000 ). Accordingly, when an AI virtual human is experienced as a socially present actor, its recommendations may be processed more like interpersonal advice. Research on livestream commerce and virtual influencers has likewise shown that higher social presence is associated with stronger purchase intention and can link characteristics of the virtual communicator to downstream consumer responses ( Chen et al., 2023 ; Zourrig et al., 2025 ). In the present study, when kinship-oriented language and sustained interaction make an AI virtual human feel socially present, its product recommendations may be more readily incorporated into an interpersonal judgment process. Higher social presence is therefore expected to be positively associated with purchase intention.

Empathy may shape subsequent persuasive responses through emotional understanding and vicarious experience. Communication research has shown that state empathy can enhance audiences’ understanding of a message character’s situation and emotions, strengthen persuasive effects, and reduce psychological reactance ( Shen, 2010a ; Shen, 2019 ). Research on short-form influencer advertising has also found positive associations between empathy, user engagement, and purchase intention, suggesting that when audiences are better able to understand and share an influencer’s emotional experience, they are more likely to exhibit favorable downstream responses ( Li B. et al., 2023 ). In the present study, caring expressions, companionship, and everyday experiences embedded in kinship-oriented narratives provide older viewers with accessible points of emotional engagement. When viewers experience stronger state empathy toward an AI virtual human, they may therefore respond more favorably to its product recommendations.

Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

 H9 : Social presence is positively associated with purchase intention.

 H10 : Empathy is positively associated with purchase intention.
 3.4. 
## Indirect associations through social presence and empathy

Existing research on influencers and virtual characters suggests that communicator characteristics do not necessarily translate directly into consumer intentions. Instead, they may first shape audiences’ social perceptions, relational responses, or emotional experiences, which are then associated with recommendation acceptance and purchase judgments. Research on virtual influencers has shown that humanlike characteristics can be linked to recommendation acceptance and purchase outcomes through social-perceptual mechanisms such as social presence. Related work on virtual streamers has likewise identified indirect associations between anthropomorphism and purchase intention through different forms of social presence ( Zourrig et al., 2025 ). Building on this literature, the present study further examines whether different antecedents are indirectly associated with purchase intention through social presence and empathy.

For loneliness, prior research on older consumers has shown that higher levels of loneliness are associated with stronger social responses to media figures. Lim and Kim (2011) found that loneliness was positively related to parasocial interaction with television shopping hosts among older consumers, and that this social response was further associated with the shopping experience. Lee and Park (2017) similarly found that older consumers could use interactions with media figures in television shopping to alleviate loneliness, with subsequent implications for consumer responses. In a digital kinship context, loneliness may therefore be indirectly associated with purchase intention through the two proximal social responses examined here.

For emotional appeal, existing research provides more direct evidence for an emotional-processing pathway. Studies of social media advertising have shown that emotional appeals can increase users’ tendency to express empathy, which is subsequently positively associated with purchase intention ( Lee and Hong, 2016 ; Demirci et al., 2024 ). This suggests that the persuasive effects of emotional content can operate through audiences’ emotional responses. Expressions of care, concern, and warmth can also function as social cues that strengthen the perceived social presence and interactivity of a technological agent. Research on virtual streamers and virtual influencers has shown that content characterized by emotional expression, interactivity, and responsiveness is associated with stronger social presence, which is in turn related to purchase intention ( Liu et al., 2025 ; Lin et al., 2024 ; Zourrig et al., 2025 ). Emotional appeal may therefore be indirectly associated with purchase intention through both social presence and empathy.

For human likeness, existing evidence suggests that social presence represents an important psychological pathway linking humanlike characteristics to consumer outcomes. Zourrig et al. (2025) found that the human likeness and animacy of virtual influencers enhanced social presence, which was further associated with recommendation acceptance and purchase intention. Lin et al. (2025) likewise showed that anthropomorphism among virtual influencers increased social presence and subsequently strengthened purchase intention. The pathway from human likeness through empathy reflects a different psychological process. In human–robot interaction research, anthropomorphic appearance has been shown to shape mind attribution, while robot appearance can also influence emotional contagion under certain conditions ( Spaccatini et al., 2023 ; Yang and Xie, 2024 ). These findings suggest that human likeness may facilitate empathic processing by making AI entities easier to interpret as social agents capable of psychological and emotional states, which may in turn be associated with consumer intentions. At the same time, prior research also indicates that this relationship is context dependent, as anthropomorphic design does not consistently increase empathy ( Roesler et al., 2021 ; Schömbs et al., 2023 ). We therefore examine this pathway as a theoretically grounded expectation derived from prior AI and human–robot interaction research.

For homophily, research on virtual influencers has found that perceived homophily and parasocial relationships can jointly mediate the relationship between influencer type and purchase intention, suggesting that perceived similarity may be linked to consumer responses by strengthening audiences’ social processing of the communicator ( Dondapati and Dehury, 2024 ). Other research on virtual influencers has shown that empathy can significantly shape users’ social attraction to and interaction with virtual characters, indicating that empathy may serve as an important emotional mechanism connecting perceptions of the character with subsequent behavioral responses ( Mirowska and Arsenyan, 2023 ).

Therefore, we propose the following hypotheses:

 H11 : Loneliness, emotional appeal, human likeness, and homophily are indirectly associated with purchase intention through social presence.

 H12 : Loneliness, emotional appeal, human likeness, and homophily are indirectly associated with purchase intention through empathy.

## Methods
 4.1. 
## Questionnaire design

The questionnaire included demographic questions together with multi-item measures for the focal constructs in the proposed model. In total, 24 items were adapted from established studies and assessed on a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). To ensure linguistic and cultural appropriateness, the original English items were translated into Chinese and then checked through back-translation, expert consultation, and pilot testing. For descriptive purposes, a composite score for each construct was calculated by averaging its constituent items. Accordingly, all composite scores had a possible range from 1 to 5, with higher scores indicating higher levels of the corresponding construct. The original item-level indicators were retained for the PLS-SEM analyses. The full wording of all measurement items is reported in Table 1 .

Loneliness was measured with three items adapted from Hughes et al. (2004) . An example item was “I feel I lack companionship.” The three-item composite demonstrated acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach’s α  = 0.759; M  = 3.940, SD  = 0.860).

Emotional appeal was measured with three items adapted from Liu and Wu (2025) . An example item was “When watching the character in this video, I feel as though the care or emotion conveyed is directed at me.” The scale demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach’s α  = 0.812; M  = 4.020, SD  = 0.790).

Human likeness was measured with three items adapted from Liu and Chen (2026) . An example item was “While watching this video, I feel that the character is alive.” The scale demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.847; M  = 3.950, SD  = 0.920).

Homophily was measured with three items adapted from Lou and Kim (2019) and Masuda et al. (2022) . An example item was “The character in this video and I have a lot in common.” The scale demonstrated acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach’s α  = 0.749; M  = 3.680, SD  = 0.960).

Social presence was measured with four items adapted from Gefen and Straub (2004) and Jiang and Chen (2024) . An example item was “When I watch videos posted by the character in this video, I feel as if I am interacting with a real person.” The scale demonstrated acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach’s α  = 0.740; M  = 4.010, SD  = 0.710).

Empathy was assessed with four items adapted from Shen (2010a , b) . An example item was “I can understand the emotions conveyed by the AI virtual human in this short video.” The scale demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach’s α  = 0.797; M  = 4.030, SD  = 0.730).

Purchase intention was measured with four items adapted from Liu et al. (2025) and Kim and Park (2023) . An example item was “I will buy the products recommended by the character in this video in the future.” The scale demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach’s α  = 0.827; M  = 4.000, SD  = 0.730).

Except for loneliness, all constructs were measured after participants had completed viewing the stimulus materials. Emotional appeal, human likeness, and homophily were treated as participants’ post-exposure perceptions and appraisals of the standardized stimulus set, rather than as objective video attributes manipulated by the researchers. Social presence, empathy, and purchase intention reflected participants’ proximal psychological responses and behavioral intentions within the same viewing context. Before the formal measures, participants were instructed to answer all subsequent items based on their overall impressions of the full set of stimulus materials.
 4.2. 
## Stimulus materials and viewing procedure

Before the formal measures, all participants completed a standardized stimulus-viewing session in which they viewed the same materials in the same order. The stimuli were selected from two AI virtual human accounts on WeChat Channels. Both accounts featured AI-generated virtual characters in dramatized narratives centered on the family life of the virtual character “Jianguo” and his son “Xiaohai.” Character appearance, tone of communication, and relationship structure were consistent across the two accounts, and all content was labeled as AI-generated. The two accounts were selected because their content simultaneously exhibited four defining features of the digital kinship context: kinship-based forms of address, family narratives, expressions of care and emotion, and companionship-oriented interaction directed toward viewers. In each video, the family narrative was followed by a product recommendation, with Jianguo introducing the product within the same emotional context and presenting it through an embedded shopping-cart link. This narrative-to-recommendation structure placed emotional communication and commercial recommendation within the same relational context, consistent with the definition of digital kinship adopted in this study.

The researchers selected three representative videos from each account, forming a standardized stimulus set of six videos. To be included, each video had to contain all four features described above, followed by a product recommendation through an embedded shopping-cart link, and had to be similar in length, with each lasting approximately 1 min. Across the six videos, the core characters, family relationship structure, kinship-oriented communication style, AI identity disclosure, narrative-to-recommendation structure, and video duration were held consistent. The videos differed only in the specific family-life episodes presented and the products recommended. The recommended products were nail clippers and tea, both relatively low-cost, low-risk everyday items. This choice was intended to reduce the influence of product-risk judgments on purchase intention so that responses would more closely reflect participants’ reactions to the recommender.

Participants first browsed the homepage of the first account to familiarize themselves with the characters, their relationships, and the overall content format, and then watched the account’s three videos in sequence. They subsequently followed the same procedure for the second account, first browsing its homepage and then viewing its three videos. This homepage-first, sequential-viewing procedure allowed participants to develop a relatively comprehensive understanding of the virtual characters’ roles, family relationships, interaction style, and product-recommendation format before completing the measures. After viewing all six videos, participants proceeded to the formal measurement section. The original questionnaire included explicit instructions requiring participants to answer all subsequent items based on their overall impressions of the two accounts and all of the videos they had just viewed. Accordingly, the unit of evaluation in this study was the complete standardized stimulus set, rather than any individual video. Expressions such as “this video,” “the person in the video,” “this character,” and “the recommended product” in the questionnaire were therefore understood, under these instructions, as referring to participants’ overall viewing experience of the preceding stimulus materials. The initial screening item asking whether participants had previously encountered AI virtual human content was used only to confirm basic experience with short-video use and did not determine the target of subsequent evaluations.
 4.3. 
## Data collection

Data were collected from February 27 to March 7, 2026, through the Chinese online survey platform Wenjuanxing. 1 The target population comprised Chinese adults aged 65 years and older. Given the potential for age misreporting, proxy responses, and low-quality participation in online surveys of older adults, participant recruitment combined targeted sampling with eligibility screening. The questionnaire was primarily distributed through online channels oriented toward older users, and age and basic demographic screening questions were presented at the survey entry. Age was entered numerically, and only participants aged 65 years or older were permitted to proceed; those who did not meet the age criterion were automatically screened out. The survey introduction also explicitly stated that the questionnaire was to be completed by eligible older participants themselves and that responses submitted on their behalf by children, other family members, or third parties were not permitted.

Multiple data-quality procedures were implemented to further improve the authenticity and reliability of the sample. First, an open-ended verification question concerning participants’ actual everyday experiences was included in the middle of the questionnaire to help identify responses that showed clear signs of proxy completion, careless responding, or inconsistency with the target population. Second, the survey platform recorded device information and completion time. Responses repeatedly submitted from the same device within a short period were examined, and anomalous duplicate cases were excluded. Third, demographic questions appearing at different points in the questionnaire were cross-checked for consistency. Responses containing clear contradictions in basic information such as age or gender were classified as invalid. An instructed-response item was also included, and respondents who failed to select the specified option were excluded. Given the combined length of the stimulus-viewing procedure and the questionnaire, surveys completed in less than 8 min were additionally treated as instances of unusually rapid responding and were included among the criteria for invalid responses.

Before participation, all respondents were informed of the study purpose, the anonymous nature of participation, and how the data would be used. Participation was voluntary. No directly identifying personal information was collected, and all data were used solely for academic research and handled anonymously during subsequent analyses. A total of 380 questionnaires were collected. After applying the eligibility and data-quality screening procedures described above, 372 valid responses remained and were included in the statistical analyses. The demographic characteristics of the final sample are presented in Table 2 .

In addition, nonresponse bias was assessed using the procedure proposed by Armstrong and Overton (1977) , whereby earlier and later respondents were compared. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in the mean scores of the principal study variables, suggesting that the likelihood of substantial nonresponse bias affecting the study findings was low.
 4.4. 
## Data analysis

This study employed partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) as two parallel and complementary analytical approaches addressing different levels of the research question. PLS-SEM was used to examine, on average, which user-level conditions and perceived cues and appraisals associated with elicited agent knowledge were related to the two proximal social responses, and how these responses were subsequently associated with purchase intention. In contrast, fsQCA was used to identify the combinations of conditions associated with high purchase intention and to assess whether conditions showing relatively weak net associations in the linear analysis could become important within particular configurations. Because the two methods are grounded in different analytical logics, their results were interpreted as complementary and mutually informative.

PLS-SEM was considered appropriate for the present study for three main reasons. First, the study has both predictive and theory-extending objectives. Drawing on sociality motivation and elicited agent knowledge from the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism and integrating them with the CASA paradigm, the model examines relationships among users’ sociality motivation, perceived cues and appraisals associated with elicited agent knowledge, proximal social responses, and behavioral intention. PLS-SEM is well suited to structural models aimed at explaining and predicting endogenous constructs ( Hair et al., 2019 ). Second, the proposed model includes four antecedent variables, two parallel proximal-response variables, and one behavioral outcome, with multiple direct and indirect paths. PLS-SEM enables the simultaneous assessment of the measurement and structural models and allows indirect associations to be evaluated through bootstrapping procedures ( Hair et al., 2017 ). Third, according to the inverse square root method proposed by Kock and Hadaya (2018) , the minimum sample size required at a 5% significance level and 80% statistical power was lower than the 372 valid cases obtained in this study, indicating that the available sample was sufficient for model estimation. As a variable-centered method based on a net-effect logic, PLS-SEM was used to estimate the conditional association of each predictor while accounting for the other antecedents in the model, as well as its average relationship with the corresponding endogenous construct.

fsQCA, by contrast, is grounded in set-theoretic logic and treats each case as a configuration of multiple conditions, focusing on how combinations of conditions may jointly constitute sufficient configurations for an outcome ( Ragin, 2008 ). Its complementarity with PLS-SEM is particularly relevant in three respects. First, fsQCA accommodates equifinality, meaning that high purchase intention may arise from multiple distinct combinations of conditions. Second, it incorporates causal asymmetry in the set-theoretic sense, such that the configurations associated with high purchase intention need not be simple mirror images of those associated with low purchase intention. Third, fsQCA emphasizes conjunctural relationships among conditions, recognizing that the importance of a given condition may vary depending on the other conditions with which it co-occurs ( Fiss, 2011 ; Pappas and Woodside, 2021 ). Accordingly, loneliness, emotional appeal, human likeness, homophily, social presence, and empathy were specified as antecedent conditions, with purchase intention treated as the outcome set. This configuration allows the analysis to identify which conditions play stable core roles across configurations associated with high purchase intention and which become important only within particular combinations.
 4.5. 
## Common method bias

Given the cross-sectional, self-reported nature of the data and the reliance on a single data source, common method bias (CMB) may be a potential concern and could affect the robustness of the findings ( Podsakoff et al., 2003 ). To reduce this risk, both procedural and statistical remedies were adopted. At the procedural level, the questionnaire was pilot-tested, and participants were allowed to respond anonymously. At the statistical level, Harman’s single-factor test was conducted. The results yielded five factors with eigenvalues greater than 1, with the first factor accounting for 38.622% of the total variance. Because no single factor accounted for the majority of the variance, the results did not indicate an obvious problem of severe common method bias ( Podsakoff et al., 2003 ). Overall, the data showed no clear evidence of substantial CMB.

## Results
 5.1. 
## Measurement model assessment

The PLS-SEM analysis was conducted following the standard two-step approach ( Hair et al., 2019 ), beginning with an evaluation of the measurement model and subsequently proceeding to the assessment of the structural relationships.

As shown in Table 1 , all constructs demonstrate acceptable internal consistency, with composite reliability and Cronbach’s alpha values above 0.70 ( Hair et al., 2019 ). In addition, all AVE values exceed 0.50, indicating adequate convergent validity ( Fornell and Larcker, 1981 ; Hair et al., 2019 ).

As shown in Table 3 , the Fornell–Larcker criterion was satisfied, as the square root of the average variance extracted (AVE) for each construct, shown on the diagonal, exceeded its correlations with all other constructs ( Fornell and Larcker, 1981 ). Discriminant validity was further assessed using the heterotrait–monotrait ratio of correlations (HTMT). As reported in Table 4 , all HTMT values were below the recommended threshold of 0.90 ( Henseler et al., 2015 ; Hair et al., 2019 ). Taken together, these results indicate satisfactory discriminant validity for all constructs.

 5.2. 
## Structural model assessment

Before examining the structural paths, multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIFs). In PLS-SEM, structural-model VIF values below 5 are generally considered acceptable ( Hair et al., 2019 ). The maximum VIF in the present study was 2.405, indicating no problematic multicollinearity among the predictor constructs.

The significance of the structural paths was assessed using a bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples. The standardized path coefficients and hypothesis-testing results are reported in Table 5 and Figure 2 . First, loneliness ( β  = 0.250, p  < 0.001), emotional appeal ( β  = 0.363, p  < 0.001), human likeness ( β  = 0.190, p  < 0.001), and homophily ( β  = 0.128, p  < 0.05) were all positively associated with social presence. Thus, H1, H3, H5, and H7 were supported.

Second, loneliness ( β  = 0.279, p  < 0.001), emotional appeal ( β  = 0.244, p  < 0.001), and homophily ( β  = 0.286, p  < 0.001) were positively associated with empathy. In the PLS-SEM framework, the coefficient of determination ( R 2 ) reflects the proportion of variance explained in each endogenous construct and is commonly used to evaluate the model’s explanatory power. Following established guidelines, R 2 values of 0.75, 0.50, and 0.25 can be interpreted as substantial, moderate, and weak, respectively ( Hair et al., 2019 ). The R 2 values for social presence (0.538), empathy (0.521), and purchase intention (0.418) indicate satisfactory explanatory performance.

To further assess the model’s out-of-sample predictive performance, the PLSpredict procedure in SmartPLS 4 was employed. PLSpredict generates out-of-sample predictions through cross-validation, with Q 2 _predict used to assess predictive relevance for the endogenous constructs. The Q 2 _predict values for social presence, empathy, and purchase intention were 0.523, 0.507, and 0.410, respectively, all exceeding zero (see Table 6 ). These results indicate that the model demonstrates predictive relevance for the three endogenous constructs.( Shmueli et al., 2019 ; Tenenhaus et al., 2005 ).

Beyond explanatory and predictive evaluation, effect sizes ( f 2 ) were also examined to determine the relative contribution of each predictor. This metric complements significance testing by indicating the magnitude of individual effects ( Hair et al., 2019 ). Based on Cohen’s (2013) benchmarks, f 2 values of 0.02, 0.15, and 0.35 correspond to small, medium, and large effects, respectively.

The results indicate that loneliness ( f 2  = 0.066), emotional appeal ( f 2  = 0.147), human likeness ( f 2  = 0.065), and homophily ( f 2  = 0.020) all have small effects on social presence. Similarly, loneliness ( f 2  = 0.080), emotional appeal ( f 2  = 0.064), and homophily ( f 2  = 0.095) exhibit small effects on empathy. Regarding purchase intention, social presence ( f 2  = 0.129) shows a small effect, while empathy ( f 2  = 0.205) demonstrates a medium effect.

SmartPLS 4.0 directly supports the assessment of indirect associations using the bootstrapping procedure. In this study, bootstrapping was conducted with 5,000 resamples. The results are presented in Table 7 . All indirect associations were statistically significant except the pathway from human likeness to purchase intention through empathy. Accordingly, H11 was supported, whereas H12 received partial support.

 5.3. 
## fsQCA

This study included six antecedent conditions in the fsQCA: loneliness, emotional appeal, human likeness, homophily, social presence, and empathy, with purchase intention specified as the outcome. The selection of these conditions was consistent with the theoretical model developed above. Loneliness represents older adults’ individual socioemotional state; emotional appeal, human likeness, and homophily capture their perceptions of the AI virtual human and its communication; and social presence and empathy reflect the relational and emotional responses that emerge during exposure. Examining these conditions jointly makes it possible to assess how different combinations of individual states, media perceptions, and psychological responses are associated with high purchase intention. The final sample comprised 372 valid cases. With six antecedent conditions, 64 theoretically possible configurations can be formed, and the available sample provided a sufficient empirical basis for examining the distribution of cases across these configurations. By focusing on conjunctural relationships and equifinal pathways, fsQCA is particularly well suited to identifying multiple combinations of conditions associated with the same outcome.

Before conducting the configurational analysis, all variables were calibrated into fuzzy sets by transforming the original scale scores into set-membership scores ranging from 0 to 1 ( Ragin, 2008 ). Following the direct calibration method and taking the empirical distributions of the variables in the present sample into account, the 95th, 50th, and 5th percentiles were used as the three qualitative anchors for full membership, the crossover point, and full non-membership, respectively. Full membership indicates a high degree of membership in the corresponding condition set, whereas full non-membership indicates a low degree of membership. The crossover point represents the point of maximum ambiguity regarding whether a case belongs to the set ( Ragin, 2008 ). The specific calibration anchors and corresponding values for each variable are reported in Table 8 . Through this procedure, loneliness, emotional appeal, human likeness, homophily, social presence, empathy, and purchase intention were converted into fuzzy-set variables suitable for subsequent set-theoretic analysis.

A necessity analysis was conducted separately for high and low purchase intention (see Table 9 ) to determine whether any individual condition could be regarded as necessary for the outcome. In fsQCA, a condition is generally considered potentially necessary when its consistency reaches or exceeds 0.90. For high purchase intention, the consistency values of all individual conditions and their negations were below 0.90. Loneliness showed the highest consistency (0.829), followed by empathy (0.818) and emotional appeal (0.798). For low purchase intention, none of the conditions or their negations reached the 0.90 threshold either. The absence of homophily showed the highest consistency (0.835), followed by the absence of emotional appeal (0.802) and the absence of empathy (0.782).

Thus, no single condition constituted a necessary condition for either high or low purchase intention. This finding suggests that older adults’ higher or lower purchase intention toward products recommended by AI virtual humans does not depend on any single factor in isolation, but is more likely to emerge from combinations of multiple conditions. On this basis, the subsequent analysis focused on sufficiency configurations to identify multiple pathways associated with high purchase intention.

A truth table was then constructed to conduct the sufficiency analysis. With six antecedent conditions, 64 theoretically possible configurations could be generated. Given the sample size of 372 valid cases and the distribution of cases across configurations, the case-frequency threshold was set at 4, meaning that only configurations containing at least four cases were retained for the subsequent sufficiency analysis. The consistency threshold was set at 0.80 to ensure a sufficiently strong set-theoretic inclusion relationship between the retained configurations and high purchase intention. In addition, the proportional reduction in inconsistency (PRI) threshold was set at 0.75 to reduce contradictory configurations that were simultaneously associated with both the presence and absence of the outcome.

The truth-table analysis generated complex, parsimonious, and intermediate solutions. The relative importance of each condition was determined by comparing the parsimonious and intermediate solutions. Conditions appearing in both solutions were identified as core conditions, whereas conditions appearing only in the intermediate solution were treated as peripheral conditions. The final interpretation primarily relied on the intermediate solution to report the configurational pathways associated with high purchase intention. The explanatory relevance of each configuration was evaluated using configuration consistency, raw coverage, and unique coverage, together with the overall solution consistency and solution coverage.

The configurational results are presented in Table 10 . The overall solution consistency was 0.903, exceeding the recommended threshold of 0.80, while the overall solution coverage was 0.688, indicating that the three configurational patterns jointly accounted for 68.8% of the membership in the high-purchase-intention set. Based on differences in core conditions, the configurations can be grouped into three patterns.

Configuration S1 was characterized by emotional appeal and empathy as core conditions and comprised three subconfigurations. Loneliness, human likeness, homophily, and social presence appeared only as peripheral conditions in some subconfigurations and were irrelevant in others. This pattern suggests that when the virtual character conveys sufficiently salient expressions of care and emotion, such affective cues can be associated with empathy and high purchase intention regardless of older adults’ level of loneliness or perceived similarity with the character.

Configuration S2 was characterized by loneliness, emotional appeal, homophily, and empathy as core conditions. Compared with S1, homophily moved into a core position, indicating that self-referential inference plays a key role in this configuration. When older adults perceive the virtual character as sharing similar values and life experiences, they may be more likely to interpret its emotional expressions as personally relevant forms of care and to experience emotional resonance, with loneliness further strengthening this pattern. S2 had a unique coverage of 0.100, the highest among all configurations, indicating that it accounted for a comparatively large proportion of cases not covered by the other configurations.

Configuration S3 was characterized by loneliness, emotional appeal, and homophily as core conditions, with human likeness and social presence as peripheral conditions and empathy as an irrelevant condition. This configuration combines sociality motivation from the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism with social cues related to elicited agent knowledge provided by the AI virtual human and its content, while the proximal social response is primarily reflected in social presence. This pattern suggests that when both anthropomorphic motivation and social cues are sufficiently present, older adults may experience the virtual character as a socially present actor and respond to its recommendations through interpersonal norms without necessarily relying on empathic resonance.

Compared with the PLS-SEM results, the fsQCA findings reveal configurational patterns beyond average net associations. Emotional appeal and empathy were not only associated with purchase intention in the linear model but also emerged as core conditions across multiple configurations leading to high purchase intention, indicating their relatively stable importance. Human likeness, by contrast, was primarily associated with social presence in the PLS-SEM results and appeared mainly as a peripheral condition in fsQCA, suggesting that its relevance is more dependent on the specific configuration in which it occurs. Moreover, some configurations did not require empathy and social presence to be simultaneously present, indicating that high purchase intention can emerge through multiple distinct psychological configurations.

To assess the robustness of the configurational results, this study re-ran the sufficiency analysis by separately adjusting the calibration anchors and the case-frequency threshold, while keeping the consistency and PRI thresholds unchanged (see Table 11 ). In Robustness Test 1, the calibration anchors were changed from the 95th, 50th, and 5th percentiles to the 90th, 50th, and 10th percentiles. This analysis yielded three configurations, with an overall solution consistency of 0.907 and solution coverage of 0.592. In Robustness Test 2, the original calibration scheme was retained, but the case-frequency threshold was increased from 4 to 5. This analysis produced four configurations, with an overall solution consistency of 0.917 and solution coverage of 0.662. In both tests, solution consistency remained above 0.90.

At the configurational level, S1 was fully retained in both robustness tests, with emotional appeal and empathy consistently remaining core conditions, indicating the strongest robustness. For S2, emotional appeal, homophily, and empathy remained core conditions across both tests, whereas loneliness shifted to a peripheral condition. In Robustness Test 1, human likeness also emerged as a core condition, suggesting that the roles of loneliness and human likeness within this configuration were relatively sensitive to the calibration criteria. S3 was reproduced after the case-frequency threshold was increased, with emotional appeal and homophily remaining core conditions, but it did not reappear after the calibration anchors were adjusted.

Overall, the combination of emotional appeal and empathy emerged as the most stable configuration associated with high purchase intention. Emotional appeal and homophily also constituted the stable core of a similar pathway, whereas loneliness primarily played an enhancing role within specific configurations.
 6. 
## Conclusion and implications
 6.1. 
## Discussion of the results

This study examined older adults’ social responses to AI virtual humans in a digital kinship context and how these responses were associated with purchase intention.

Loneliness was positively associated with both social presence and empathy. Prior research has shown that lonely individuals often display heightened vigilance toward social threat and greater withdrawal in face-to-face interaction ( Cacioppo and Hawkley, 2009 ). In the present study, however, older adults with higher levels of loneliness exhibited stronger social responses to AI virtual humans. This pattern is consistent with research on social reconnection, which suggests that unmet affiliation needs may be directed toward targets that involve little or no risk of rejection ( Maner et al., 2007 ). One-way short-video viewing provides precisely such a context. The need for social connection has been identified as an important motivation for attributing social qualities to nonhuman entities ( Epley et al., 2008b ), and Lim and Kim (2011) similarly found that lonely older adults were more likely to develop parasocial interaction with television shopping hosts. The present findings extend this relationship to AI virtual humans. The fsQCA results further qualify this pattern. Loneliness emerged as a core condition only in configurations in which homophily was also core and became a peripheral condition in the robustness tests. This suggests that the need for social connection may function more as an amplifying condition: it appears more likely to translate into stronger social responses and purchase intention when the target is also perceived as relevant to the self in terms of experience and values.

 Hu et al. (2020) found that lonely individuals were more inclined to engage in empathic situations that could provide a sense of social support. The stimuli used in the present study likewise contained explicit cues of social connection. Through kinship-based forms of address, direct self-disclosure, caring expressions, and requests for companionship, the virtual characters positioned viewers in a relational role in which they could feel both addressed and needed. For older adults experiencing greater loneliness, these expressions not only provided cues for understanding the character’s emotions and circumstances but also reinforced meanings of acceptance and connection. This may have made empathic understanding and emotional resonance more likely. The association between loneliness and empathy should therefore be interpreted in relation to the specific social context in which it occurs. When an AI virtual human’s emotional expression simultaneously communicates care, companionship, and an invitation to connect, loneliness-related sociality motivation may be more readily translated into empathic responding.

Emotional appeal and homophily were both positively associated with social presence and empathy, but they showed different patterns of association. Emotional appeal was more strongly associated with social presence ( β  = 0.363) than with empathy ( β  = 0.244), whereas homophily showed a stronger association with empathy ( β  = 0.286) than with social presence ( β  = 0.128). These patterns are consistent with the different types of information conveyed by the two cues. Emotional appeal provides information about the character’s current emotional state and relational intentions. Expressions of personal concern, greetings, and care may make an AI virtual human easier to experience as a social actor actively participating in an interaction, consistent with experimental evidence that emotional cues from artificial agents can enhance social presence ( Zhang et al., 2024 ). Homophily, in contrast, increases perceived similarity between the character and the self, making viewers’ own experiences more applicable when interpreting the character’s psychological state ( Epley et al., 2007 ). Emotional appeal therefore provides emotional information to be processed, whereas homophily increases the comparability of that information with the viewer’s own experience. Prior research on virtual influencers has similarly linked perceived similarity to stronger relational responses ( Stein et al., 2024 ). The present findings suggest that self-similarity may serve as an important cognitive reference point through which older adults understand the emotions and circumstances of AI virtual humans.

Human likeness showed a different pattern from the other two social cues. It was positively associated with social presence ( β  = 0.190), but its association with empathy did not reach statistical significance ( β  = 0.081, p  = 0.061). This result suggests that humanlike presentation is more consistently related to perceptions of social presence, whereas its relationship with empathy may be more context dependent. Epley et al. (2007) argue that humanlike appearance and behavior increase the accessibility of human-related knowledge when interpreting nonhuman entities, and related research has shown that human likeness can enhance the social presence of artificial agents ( Li Q. et al., 2023 ). By contrast, in the experiment by Riek et al. (2009) , observers had to infer whether robots were capable of feeling largely from their appearance, making human likeness an important cue for empathy. In the present study, however, the virtual characters explicitly communicated loneliness and a need for companionship through language, meaning that their emotional states were already made salient by the content itself. Recent research similarly suggests that context-relevant self-disclosure can promote empathy toward artificial agents, whereas the contribution of visual anthropomorphism may be comparatively limited ( Tsumura and Yamada, 2023 ). When emotional information is already explicit, human likeness may therefore contribute primarily to social presence while adding relatively little to empathic processing.

The relatively weak association between human likeness and empathy may also reflect additional perceptual responses elicited by highly humanlike AI. Prior research has shown that attributing experiential capacities to humanlike machines can sometimes evoke discomfort ( Gray and Wegner, 2012 ), while emotional expression by autonomous AI characters may be perceived as more uncanny than the same expression by human characters ( Stein and Ohler, 2017 ). Thus, greater animacy, perceived realness, and behavioral naturalness may enhance the social perceptibility of a virtual character while simultaneously drawing attention to artificiality, scripted expression, or human–machine incongruity. These factors were not directly measured in the present study, and future research could examine whether they shape the relationship between human likeness and empathy.

Both social presence and empathy were significantly associated with purchase intention, with empathy showing the larger coefficient ( β  = 0.410) and social presence a somewhat smaller one ( β  = 0.325). CASA proposes that once a technological object is incorporated into a social interaction framework, people apply interpersonal norms and judgment rules to it ( Nass and Moon, 2000 ). The association between social presence and purchase intention can therefore be understood as a process in which product recommendations are evaluated within a frame resembling advice from a socially present actor, consistent with findings from livestream commerce and virtual influencer research ( Chen et al., 2023 ; Zourrig et al., 2025 ). The relatively stronger association involving empathy further suggests that, in kinship-oriented contexts, the interpersonal norm activated may be closer to relational responsiveness. When viewers understand and share the feelings of a character who addresses them in a family-like role and discloses personal concerns, responding favorably to that character’s recommendation may be experienced as a response to the relationship itself rather than solely as an evaluation of product information. State empathy has been shown to facilitate message acceptance and reduce psychological reactance ( Shen, 2010a , 2019 ). In commercial settings, this raises the possibility that emotional resonance may reduce older adults’ scrutiny of persuasive motives. Because persuasion knowledge and psychological reactance were not measured in the present study, this possibility should be examined directly in future research.

The results of the two analytical approaches were broadly consistent and complementary. Emotional appeal and empathy showed significant associations in the PLS-SEM results and also emerged as the most frequent and stable core conditions in the fsQCA solutions, indicating that both played consistently important roles at the variable and configurational levels. The association between human likeness and empathy was not supported in the PLS-SEM analysis. Likewise, human likeness never appeared as a core condition in the baseline fsQCA configurations, but only as a peripheral condition in selected solutions, including S3, where it appeared alongside social presence. This pattern is consistent with the finding that human likeness was primarily associated with social presence. The fsQCA results also revealed two features that could not be captured by the PLS-SEM analysis. First, the core role of loneliness depended on the simultaneous presence of homophily, suggesting that sociality motivation may become more consequential when the target is perceived as self-relevant. Second, S3 showed that high purchase intention could emerge even when empathy was irrelevant, illustrating equifinality and causal asymmetry at the configurational level. These patterns cannot be examined through the symmetric net-effect logic of PLS-SEM, although S3 was relatively sensitive to changes in the calibration criteria.

Overall, the three perceived cues and appraisals associated with elicited agent knowledge provide a useful basis for understanding AI-mediated persuasion in digital kinship contexts. Human likeness is primarily associated with whether an AI virtual human is perceived as a socially present actor; emotional appeal provides information about the character’s emotional state and relational intentions; and homophily connects these emotions and experiences to viewers’ own life experiences. At the same time, empathy was more strongly associated with purchase intention than social presence, suggesting that consumer responses to kinship-oriented AI communication are closely related to viewers’ understanding of the character’s emotions and circumstances. Taken together, perceptions of social presence, understanding of the character’s emotions, and perceived similarity to one’s own experience jointly constitute the relational context within which product recommendations are processed in kinship-oriented AI content.

This relational and emotional mode of processing carries particular theoretical significance for older adults. Socioemotional selectivity theory suggests that older adults place greater value on emotionally meaningful relationships and information ( Carstensen et al., 1999 ). In the present study, loneliness was significantly associated with both social presence and empathy, indicating that older adults’ real-world state of social connectedness is involved in how they socially process AI virtual humans. When kinship-oriented content provides cues of social connection through expressions of care, requests for companionship, and familiar life experiences, older adults with stronger needs for social connection may be more likely to develop relational and emotional responses and to process product recommendations within this interactional context. The consumer responses associated with kinship-oriented AI content should therefore be understood within a broader framework of human–AI social connection and emotional communication, which also brings its potential implications for older consumer protection into sharper focus.
 6.2. 
## Theoretical implications

First, by integrating sociality motivation and elicited agent knowledge from the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism with the CASA paradigm, this study develops a layered framework linking user-level psychological conditions, perceptions and appraisals of AI virtual humans, proximal social responses, and behavioral intentions. Existing research on AI virtual humans and virtual influencers has largely focused on character-related features or communication outcomes, with less attention to audiences’ state of social connectedness alongside different forms of perceived cues and appraisals. The present study positions loneliness at the level of sociality motivation and, from the perspective of elicited agent knowledge, distinguishes human likeness, emotional appeal, and homophily as three forms of perception and appraisal. In doing so, it connects two related questions: how nonhuman entities acquire social meaning and how users respond to socially framed technological agents. The findings further suggest that perceptions of an AI virtual human’s social presence are more closely associated with cues conveyed by the character, whereas emotional understanding depends more strongly on emotional content and viewers’ own self-relevant experiences.

Second, this study extends research on older adults’ digital media use by bringing social connection needs and human–AI social responses into greater focus. Prior work has often explained older adults’ digital behavior in terms of digital competence, technology adoption, platform use, and information seeking, while giving less attention to how real-world social connectedness may shape the social processing of AI-mediated characters. By incorporating loneliness into a human–AI social perception framework, the present study shows that older adults’ need for social connection is involved in both their perceptions of AI virtual humans as socially present and their emotional responses to them. In one-way AI-mediated content that involves little risk of interpersonal rejection, this need may be more likely to manifest as social approach. This perspective broadens research on older adults and AI beyond technology use toward social connection, relational perception, and socioemotional processing, while also extending the empirical base of virtual-influencer research beyond younger users and general commercial contexts.

Third, this study extends the understanding of AI-mediated persuasion by examining both net associations and configurational patterns. PLS-SEM reveals the average associations between different psychological conditions and social responses, whereas fsQCA further shows that high purchase intention can arise from multiple combinations of conditions and that the importance of any given condition varies depending on the other conditions with which it co-occurs. Together, the two analyses indicate that older adults’ consumer responses to kinship-oriented AI content emerge from configurations of social connection needs, perceptions and appraisals associated with elicited agent knowledge, and proximal social responses, making any single cue or pathway insufficient to fully account for these responses. These findings extend the study of commercial communication by AI virtual humans beyond linear persuasion relationships toward a framework that accommodates relational and emotional processing as well as multiple pathways to the same outcome.
 6.3. 
## Practical implications

This study offers practical implications for the governance, communication design, and protection of older users in relation to kinship-oriented AI virtual human content.

First, short-video platforms should pay greater attention to AI-generated content in which kinship roles, sustained expressions of care, and commercial recommendations appear together. The findings indicate that emotional appeal and homophily are indirectly associated with purchase intention through social presence and empathy, and that high purchase intention can emerge from multiple combinations of social and emotional conditions. Platform-level risk assessment should therefore move beyond isolated technical features and consider how kinship-based forms of address, family narratives, caring expressions, and product recommendations are combined within the same content. For AI virtual humans that repeatedly adopt roles such as children or other family members while making commercial recommendations, platforms could strengthen disclosure of AI-generated identity, commercial intent, and promotional content, while establishing clearer review standards for content that uses kinship roles to reinforce persuasive appeals. Disclosures aimed at older users should also minimize technical jargon and communicate the AI identity and commercial purpose of the virtual character in clear and accessible language.

Second, this study offers practical guidance for the use of social and emotional cues in the design of AI virtual human content. Emotional appeal and homophily were both associated with social presence and empathy, suggesting that warm expressions, shared life experiences, and perceived value similarity are related to stronger social and emotional responses toward virtual characters. In commercial communication, however, clear boundaries should be maintained between these relational cues and product recommendations. This is particularly important when kinship roles are employed, as expressions of care, concern, and companionship should not be directly converted into reasons for purchase. Human likeness was significantly associated only with social presence, while its association with empathy did not reach statistical significance, indicating that greater visual and behavioral realism does not necessarily correspond to stronger emotional responses. Content design may therefore benefit more from emphasizing information quality, appropriateness of expression, and interactional transparency than from simply pursuing higher levels of human likeness.

Finally, the protection of older users should address both digital recognition skills and real-world social connection. Loneliness was significantly associated with both social presence and empathy, indicating that older adults’ state of social connectedness is involved in how they process kinship-oriented AI content. Digital literacy initiatives could therefore extend beyond teaching users how to identify AI-generated content to include recognition of kinship-oriented language, emotional persuasion, and the relationship between relational communication and commercial recommendation. Such efforts may help older users better understand the social role and commercial purpose of virtual characters. Communities, families, and public service organizations can also broaden older adults’ sources of informational and emotional support through digital learning, intergenerational communication, and opportunities for social participation, thereby reducing overreliance on a single AI-mediated relationship.
 6.4. 
## Limitations and future research suggestions

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study relied on cross-sectional self-report data. Although all participants viewed the same standardized AI virtual human stimuli before completing the measures, loneliness, social presence, empathy, and purchase intention were assessed at a single time point. The current findings therefore primarily reflect associations among the variables and do not establish a definitive causal sequence. Future research could employ experimental designs that independently manipulate cues such as kinship-based forms of address, emotional appeal, human likeness, or homophily, and combine these designs with longitudinal tracking or multi-wave measurement to examine the development of social presence and empathy and their subsequent behavioral consequences. Although the present study standardized participants’ exposure through the same account-homepage and video-viewing procedure, the design still involved a one-time exposure and therefore cannot fully capture how relationships with the same AI virtual human may evolve through repeated and prolonged encounters on real-world platforms.

Second, this study conceptualized digital kinship as a communication context constituted by kinship roles, family narratives, caring expressions, and companionship-oriented interaction, and operationalized this context through the stimulus materials rather than measuring digital kinship as an independent psychological construct. The findings therefore illuminate older adults’ social perceptions and emotional responses within a kinship-oriented AI communication context, but they cannot determine the extent to which participants actually regarded the virtual character as a family member or developed enduring kinship identification, relational intimacy, or emotional attachment. Future research could retain digital kinship as the broader communication context while additionally measuring constructs such as perceived kinship role, perceived intimacy, caregiving obligation, relational identification, or parasocial relationships. This would help distinguish contextual cues from the relational experiences that users actually develop and allow the relationship between the two to be examined more directly.

Third, some constructs could be measured with greater conceptual granularity. Human likeness in the present study was primarily assessed through perceived animacy, realness of existence, and behavioral naturalness, capturing an overall perception of how humanlike the AI virtual character appeared. This operationalization captures general human likeness but does not distinguish among visual resemblance, behavioral naturalness, and perceptions of psychological or mental capacities. The finding that human likeness was significantly associated with social presence but not with empathy also suggests that different dimensions of perceived humanness may correspond to different socio-psychological responses. Future research could therefore distinguish visual, behavioral, and psychological dimensions of human likeness and incorporate constructs such as mind perception, perceived authenticity, or perceived artificiality to provide a more fine-grained account of how humanlike presentation relates to social presence and empathic responses.

Finally, the sample and stimulus context impose boundaries on the generalizability of the findings. Participants were Chinese internet users aged 65 years and older recruited through targeted online channels and eligibility screening, meaning that older adults who make limited use of smart devices or short-video platforms may be underrepresented. In addition, the stimuli were drawn from two AI virtual human accounts with similar character settings and content styles and centered on father–son family narratives, expressions of loneliness, and recommendations for low-risk everyday products. Virtual characters differing in age, gender, or family role, as well as settings involving health products, financial products, or other higher-risk consumption contexts, may elicit different social judgments and emotional responses. Future studies should replicate the findings across different platforms, cultural settings, and product categories, and compare distinct kinship roles, such as child, partner, and sibling roles, to assess the extent to which the relationships identified here generalize across a broader range of AI virtual human contexts.
