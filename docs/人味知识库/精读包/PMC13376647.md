## Abstract

sc-ChromAging, a chromatin accessibility-based aging clock, was developed using single-cell ATAC-seq from 401 Chinese individuals. It identified CD4⁺ naive T cells as the most accurate predictors of age. This clock linked immune aging with pathways in inflammation, infection, and tumor susceptibility, and connecting chromatin changes to plasma metabolites like triacylglycerols.

Global population aging presents a profound public health challenge 1 , necessitating a deeper understanding of the biological deterioration that accompanies advancing age. The aging process in humans constitutes a complex progression that exerts widespread effects across various organ systems, with the immune system displaying particularly significant dysregulation. This deterioration of immune integrity, often termed immunosenescence 2 , is intrinsically linked to an attenuated capacity for tissue regeneration, a heightened vulnerability to infectious diseases, and the disruption of systemic homeostasis, all of which facilitate the pathogenesis of age-associated morbidities 3 – 5 . While chronological age serves as a rough proxy for these changes, it often fails to capture the substantial heterogeneity in health trajectories among individuals 6 . Consequently, the quantification of biological age through molecular biomarkers has emerged as a pivotal strategy to assess aging status and predict health outcomes 7 – 10 .
