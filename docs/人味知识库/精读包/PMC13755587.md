## Discussion

BCI technology, as a disruptive innovation in the field of neural engineering, is being explored across the chain of diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation of neurodegenerative diseases, with the strongest evidence so far in assistive communication and adaptive neuromodulation. This systematic review shows that BCI technology, by constructing a two-way communication bridge between the brain and the device, not only offers a potentially valuable adjunct for the earlier detection of major neurological diseases such as AD, PD and ALS, but also points toward a paradigm shift from passive symptom management toward active neural function remodeling—a shift that is currently supported mainly by early-phase clinical evidence. Its core value lies in upgrading the traditional “observation-intervention” model to an intelligent closed-loop system of “monitoring-analysis-intervention-feedback”, providing a new path for tackling the global public health challenge of neurodegenerative diseases. However, its transformation from laboratory research to routine clinical application is still in the experimental stage and is constrained by a series of multifaceted challenges. Key technical challenges include improving the biocompatibility and long-term stability of implant materials, while improving electrode and sensor design to achieve more reliable neural signal acquisition. In addition, there is an urgent need to achieve greater system miniaturization and portability while improving the overall reliability and operational stability of the device. At the computational level, research focuses on optimizing signal processing algorithms to improve the accuracy and speed of neural decoding, ensuring real-time performance in practical applications.
