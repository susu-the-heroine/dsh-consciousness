## Discussion

The science of implanted memories is extremely important to the law. Within a court setting, jurors may have misconceptions about the nature of memory and be swayed by emotions or the conviction with which an individual recounts an incident, despite the fact that conviction and emotional response are not good indicators of the veracity of a memory (McNally, 2003 ; McNally et al., 2004 ). As such, experts in psychological science are often relied upon to clarify the nature of memory, memory distortion, confabulation and false memories (Otgaar et al., 2022 ). The previous discussion outlines the large body of research examining not only the phenomena of memory reconstruction and false memories, but also contextual factors that can serve as explanatory mechanisms for their manifestation.

If the decades of research in this field have taught us anything, it is that complexity and nuance in the research must be considered in expert testimony on the reconstructive nature of memory and false memories. Considering the amount of criticism the original Lost in the Mall study garnered (Blizard & Shaw, 2019 ; Crook & Dean, 1999 ; Sinason, 2022 ), it is wholly insufficient for experts to report only on the original research findings without exploration and analysis of more recent data in the field of psychological science on memory. Additionally, expert witnesses should be aware of and ready to face cross-examination on criticisms of methodology, to include those in the original Lost in the Mall study through the most recent replication by Murphy, Dawson, et al. ( 2023 ) and those studies that reanalyse its data.
